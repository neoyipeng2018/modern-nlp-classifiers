What makes a slice a slice

Rules for a slice

Decided
  1. A slice ends with a number, not with code. If the slice produced no registry row, it is not finished.
  2. A slice ends with a decision that could go either way. A slice whose gate cannot fail is a task list wearing a gate.
  3. A slice is vertical. It touches data, code, evaluation and the write-up. A slice that only adds a loader is horizontal and it hides its own risk.
  4. The frozen test split stays closed until the last step of slice 4.
  5. Cheapest and most informative first. Money and GPU time come later than the things that need neither.

What the slice found

  • The majority baseline scores 0.62 accuracy on 0.25 macro-F1. That gap is why macro-F1 is primary and accuracy is secondary.
  • Henry beats Loughran-McDonald by fourteen points, and the word counts explain most of it. Loughran-McDonald carries 140 positive stems against 893 negative ones, because it was built to catch risk language in 10-K filings. On news it over-calls negative. Henry is balanced at 83 and 72.
  • Both word lists abstain on about two rows in three, and every silent row is called neutral. Most of their "neutral" is the absence of a decision.
  • Financial PhraseBank ships 4,846 lines. Eight sentences repeat, two carry contradicting labels between copies and are dropped, 4,836 remain, and 59 fall into near-duplicate clusters.
  • Zero of 1,111 FiQA sentences carry two aspects with opposite polarity. That count was not knowable in advance and it matters for slice 2. See the warning there.

One open item from this slice. The Henry word list in the repository is transcribed from published reproductions and has not been checked against the original paper. It is graded Reported for that reason. Check it against the paper, or drop the list.

Slice 3

The bench

Priced, not run

The first slice that needs a GPU starts with the small tier. A provisional small winner validates the full development-side pipeline before the large tier starts. Each tier ends with one unified winner or an explicit no-winner result.

Warning. A tier can have no winner. A tie inside one cell is accepted. A loss in another cell is not averaged away. If no candidate ranks first or ties for first in all three cells, stop that tier and continue development. Do not add seeds to force a winner.

Step 8 can end later data work before it starts. If the learning curve is flat at 3,000 rows, then more rows do not help, and both weak supervision and Track D fail before any money is spent. That is a cheap finding and it is worth having early.

The reasoning behind the sequence

Why this order

One rule

Cheapest and most informative first. Each slice is allowed to end the project, and the earlier a slice can do that, the less it costs to find out.

  • Slice 1 before everything, because a word-list floor costs nothing and it is the number every reader checks first.
  • Slice 2 before the GPU, because the multi-target count decides whether the central claim of the project can be measured at all. Finding that out after the bench would waste the bench.
  • The power calculation before the bench, because runs that cannot detect the required gap are wasted runs.
  • The small tier before the large tier, because the cheaper family validates the full development-side pipeline. The frozen test split stays closed during this dry run.
  • The learning curve before Track D, because a flat curve means no amount of extra data helps and the whole of slice 4 collapses to distillation.
  • The test split last, because it can only be opened once and every earlier peek makes the final number a little less true.
